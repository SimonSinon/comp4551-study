Sequence & MoE Parallelism

Prerequisites
  • Lesson 5 (TP, DP).
  • Lesson 3 (attention shape O(N²)).
By the end you can:
  • Explain why sequence parallelism is needed for long contexts.
  • Describe Ring Attention and what it communicates.
  • Define MoE: gating, top-k, capacity factor, expert parallelism.
  • Identify when All-to-All comms are triggered in MoE.

1. Concepts

Why sequence parallelism (SP)?

Activation memory scales with sequence length N. Modern context windows (32k–1M tokens) make activations the dominant memory cost — even with TP, the LayerNorm and dropout activations remain replicated and large.

Megatron-SP splits these along the sequence dimension across the TP group, exchanged via cheap AllGather/ReduceScatter at TP boundaries. Net: activation memory drops roughly N/TP.

Ring Attention (for very long contexts)

For sequences too long for a single GPU even after SP, split tokens across GPUs in a ring. Each GPU holds its chunk of Q, K, V; it then rotates its K, V chunk around the ring, computing partial attention against each visiting chunk and combining results with online softmax.

Communication overlaps with computation; total data sent ≈ KV size × (ring length − 1). Used by frameworks training million-token contexts.

Mixture of Experts (MoE) — sparse activation

Replace each dense FFN with E experts (each its own FFN) plus a tiny gating network that, per token, picks the top-k experts (typically k=1 or 2). Only the chosen experts compute → params grow but FLOPs per token stay small.

gate_logits = W_gate · x (shape: E) top-k indices & weights = topk(softmax(gate_logits), k) y = Σ_{i∈top-k} weight_i · Expert_i(x)

Example: Mixtral 8×7B has 8 experts, k=2 → 13B active params per token but ~47B total.

Capacity factor & load-balancing loss

Each expert is allocated a fixed buffer (capacity = C·N·k/E). If too many tokens are routed to one expert, the surplus is dropped (skipped through residual). C > 1 (e.g., 1.25) gives slack.

To prevent collapse (one expert hogging everything), an auxiliary load-balancing loss encourages uniform routing.

Expert parallelism & All-to-All

Place different experts on different GPUs (the EP dimension). Each token must travel to wherever its chosen experts live, then results return.

This is implemented with two All-to-All collectives per MoE layer:

  1. Dispatch: send token activations to expert GPUs.
  2. Combine: send expert outputs back to original token GPU.

All-to-All is the heaviest comm pattern in MoE training and is the main reason MoE inference is tricky.

Real-world: DeepSeek-V3 / Mixtral / Qwen-MoE all use top-2 (or fine-grained top-k) gating with auxiliary balance losses and EP across nodes.

2. Socratic check

Why doesn't pure TP solve the long-context activation memory problem?

TP splits weights, not activations along the sequence dim. The LayerNorm/residual/dropout activations stay full-length on every TP rank. SP fixes this by splitting along N.

What's the key idea of Ring Attention?

Rotate K/V chunks around a ring of GPUs while each GPU holds its Q chunk fixed; combine partial attention outputs with online softmax. Communication overlaps with compute, enabling contexts that don't fit on one device.

If MoE has more parameters but the same FLOPs per token, what is the trade-off vs a dense model?

You gain capacity (parameters) cheaply in compute, but pay in memory, communication (All-to-All), and routing complexity. Inference batching is also harder because experts have variable load.

What happens when an expert's capacity is exceeded?

Excess tokens are dropped — the expert simply doesn't process them; they pass through via the residual connection. Capacity factor C>1 provides slack.

Key Takeaways

  • SP splits activations along the sequence axis → long-context training feasible.
  • Ring Attention rotates K/V chunks for sequences too long for one GPU.
  • MoE: tiny gate picks top-k of E experts per token → cheap FLOPs, huge params.
  • Load-balancing loss + capacity factor prevent collapse and overflow.
  • Expert parallelism → 2 All-to-All per MoE layer; the dominant comm cost.
