1. Concepts — Alignment

Why alignment?

A pretrained LLM is good at next-token prediction but not at being helpful. Alignment turns a raw LM into an instruction-following, safe assistant.

Stage 1 — Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

Fine-tune on curated (prompt, ideal-response) pairs with the standard next-token loss. Teaches format and instruction following. Often uses LoRA.

Stage 2 — Reward Model (RM)

Collect pairs of responses (a, b) for the same prompt with a human preference label (a ≻ b). Train a model r_θ(prompt, response) so that r_θ(prompt, a) > r_θ(prompt, b), using the Bradley–Terry loss:

L_RM = −log σ(r(p, a) − r(p, b))

r is usually the SFT model with a scalar reward head.

Stage 3 — PPO (RLHF)

Use the RM as the reward signal to RL-fine-tune the SFT model. Proximal Policy Optimization keeps the policy near the original (KL penalty) to avoid mode collapse and reward hacking.

objective = E[r(p, y)] − β · KL(π_θ ‖ π_ref)

Trains 4 networks simultaneously (policy, ref, RM, value) → expensive and unstable.

DPO — Direct Preference Optimization

Skip the explicit RM and PPO. Show that the optimal RLHF policy has a closed-form relation to the reference, and rewrite the preference loss directly on the policy:

L_DPO = −log σ( β · [log π(a|p)/π_ref(a|p) − log π(b|p)/π_ref(b|p)] )

Just a classification loss on preference pairs. Much simpler and more stable than PPO, similar or better quality.

GRPO (DeepSeek-R1)

Group-Relative Policy Optimization: like PPO but estimates advantage from a group of sampled responses per prompt (no value network needed). Cheaper, used for o1-style reasoning training.

Reward hacking

Policy finds prompts that maximise the RM but a human would dislike (e.g., always starts with "As an AI…", uses fluffy hedging). The KL penalty and frequent RM refreshes mitigate this.

Concepts — Evaluation

Benchmark families

  • MMLU: multiple-choice across 57 academic subjects. General knowledge / reasoning.
  • GSM8K: grade-school math word problems. Step-by-step reasoning.
  • MATH: competition-level math. Much harder.
  • HumanEval / MBPP: code generation, scored by pass@k (% of problems solved in k samples).
  • MT-Bench: open-ended multi-turn questions, judged by GPT-4 (LLM-as-judge).
  • Chatbot Arena: human pairwise voting → Elo ratings. Considered the gold standard for chat quality.
  • HELM, BIG-Bench: broad meta-benchmarks.

pass@k formula

pass@k = 1 − C(n−c, k) / C(n, k)

where n = #samples generated, c = #correct.

LLM-as-judge pitfalls

  • Position bias: judges favour the first (or last) response.
  • Length bias: favours longer answers.
  • Self-preference: a model judging itself rates itself higher.
  • Contamination: the judge has seen the test prompt.

Data contamination

Benchmark questions leak into training data → inflated scores. Mitigations: held-out test sets, time-cutoff testing, decontamination filtering by n-gram overlap.

Real-world: DeepSeek-R1 was trained with GRPO + rule-based rewards (correct answer + correct format) instead of a learned RM, demonstrating RL alignment without a separate reward model.

2. Socratic check

Why include a KL penalty in PPO?

To stop the policy from drifting too far from the reference (SFT) model. Without it, the policy exploits the RM's flaws (reward hacking) and loses general capability.

What does DPO actually optimise — a reward, or preferences directly?

Preferences directly. DPO derives that the optimal RLHF policy implies a particular log-ratio relationship, then minimises a classification loss on preference pairs. No explicit RM, no rollouts.

Why is Chatbot Arena often trusted more than MMLU?

MMLU is a fixed multiple-choice test — easy to contaminate, doesn't measure real chat quality. Arena uses live human pairwise votes on diverse prompts, giving a more robust quality signal (Elo).

An LLM-as-judge consistently rates the response on the left higher. What is happening?

Position bias. Mitigate by randomising order or running each pair twice with swapped positions and averaging.

Key Takeaways

  • RLHF = SFT → RM (Bradley–Terry) → PPO (with KL to reference).
  • DPO = preference loss directly on policy; no RM, no rollouts.
  • GRPO = PPO minus value network, used in DeepSeek-R1.
  • Reward hacking ⇒ keep KL penalty + refresh RM.
  • Benchmarks: MMLU (knowledge), GSM8K/MATH (reasoning), HumanEval (code, pass@k), MT-Bench/Arena (chat).
  • Beware contamination, LLM-judge biases (position, length, self-preference).
